# Japan Country Skill (`japan/SKILL.md`) — Universal Parameterized Edition

## 1. Purpose & Execution Model
This skill is a **universal, input-driven decision engine** for planning trips to Japan for **any traveler profile**. It takes a structured external user input (`TripBrief`), applies Japan-specific domain rules, queries curated local sources (Tabelog, Japan-Guide, Ikyu, Reddit Trip Reports, tactical YouTube creators) and live APIs (Apify/SerpApi hotel inventory, Google Maps Places v1 & Routes v2), and outputs a verified, low-friction itinerary.

## 3. Source Hierarchy & Signal-to-Noise Rules

Regardless of user profile, always query and weight Japan sources in this strict order:

### Tier 1 (S-Tier): The Ground-Truth Databases
1. **Tabelog (`tabelog.com` — Native Restaurant Truth Layer):**
   * **Calibration Rule:** Japanese diners grade strictly. On Tabelog, **`3.00–3.25` is good**, **`3.50+` is exceptional (top ~3% nationwide)**, and **`4.00+` is world-class destination dining**.
   * **Award Filters:** Use **Tabelog Hyakumeiten (Top 100)** for specialist categories (Ramen, Sushi, Soba, Udon, Tonkatsu, Yakitori, Unagi, Curry, Bakeries, Kissaten) and **Bronze / Silver / Gold** for fine dining.
   * **Google Maps Cross-Check Rule:** Never trust raw Google Maps star ratings (`4.8★`) for restaurants in tourist zones (Shinjuku, Shibuya, Asakusa, Gion, Dotonbori)—they are heavily inflated by first-time tourists. **Use Tabelog to filter food quality (`>= 3.45`) and Google Maps API v1 only to verify opening hours, exact floor numbers (`B1F–9F`), GPS pins, and recent review keywords.**
2. **Japan-Guide (`japan-guide.com` — Sightseeing & Transit Encyclopedia):**
   * Authoritative source for attraction hours, closed days, renovation alerts, regional rail pass calculators, and physical on-the-ground *sakura* (cherry blossom) and *koyo* (autumn foliage) reports.
3. **Reddit Post-Trip Reports (`r/JapanTravel` [flair: `Trip Report`], `r/JapanTravelTips`, `r/Tokyo` megathreads, `r/finedining`, `r/ramen`):**
   * Best source for real-world friction in the last 30–90 days (queue lengths, ticket machine rules, crowd bottlenecks, pacing regret).
4. **Official Seasonal & Visibility Trackers:**
   * **Japan Meteorological Corporation (`n-kishou.com`)**, **Weathernews (`weathernews.jp`)**, and **Tenki.jp (`tenki.jp`)** for blossom/foliage dates.
   * **Mt. Fuji Visibility Forecast (`fuji-san.info`)** for 7-day cloud-cover predictions.

### Tier 2 (A-Tier): Specialist Directories, Newsletters & Tactical Creators
* **Food & Bars:** Michelin Bib Gourmand Japan, Eater 38 / Culinary Backstreets Tokyo, 50 Best Discovery (bars/dining), **TableCheck**, **Pocket Concierge**, **OMAKASE (`omakase.in`)**.

### Matrix B: Onsen & Tattoo Decision Tree

Map `wants_onsen_experience`, `onsen_privacy_preference`, and `tattoo_status` using this strict decision tree:

1. **If `tattoo_status == "visible_tattoos"`:**
   * Warn the user that **~85% of public communal onsens and hotel bathhouses in Japan prohibit visible tattoos**.
   * **Solutions to Route To:**
     * **Option 1:** Book a room with an **In-Room Private Open-Air Onsen (`客室露天風呂`)** or a hotel with **Private Lockable Couples/Family Baths (`貸切風呂` / *Kashikiri*)**—tattoos are 100% allowed inside private rooms and *kashikiri* baths.
     * **Option 2:** Route to **Kinosaki Onsen** (Hyogo — 2.5 hrs from Kyoto, where **all 7 historic public bathhouses are 100% tattoo-friendly**), **Hoshino Resorts KAI** properties (with tattoo stickers), or verify public bathhouses on **`tattoo-friendly.jp`**.
2. **If `tattoo_status == "small_coverable_with_patch"`:**
   * Advise buying waterproof skin-colored **tattoo cover stickers (`タトゥー隠しシール`)** (8×10 cm) at Don Quijote or Amazon Japan—many hotel baths (including *Dormy Inn*) allow entry if a small tattoo is completely covered by a single patch.
3. **If `onsen_privacy_preference == "in_room_private_rotenburo"`:**
   * Filter strictly on **Ikyu.com** (`Rooms with open-air baths available: ○`) and verify whether the room bath uses **real thermal spring water (`温泉`)** vs. heated tap water (`沸かし湯`).
4. **If `onsen_privacy_preference == "private_reservable_kashikiri"`:**
   * Distinguish between:
     * **Free 24/7 Walk-In *Kashikiri* Hotels (Best Value):** Kyoritsu Resort properties (`La Vista Fuji Kawaguchiko`, `Kyoto Umekoji Kadensho`, `Kyoto Arashiyama Onsen Kadensho`, `Hakone Setsugetsuka`) offer **3 to 5 themed private baths free for all overnight guests with no reservation required**.
     * **Paid Hourly *Kashikiri* Hotels (~¥3,000–¥4,500 / 50 mins):** Reserved at check-in (*Konansou*, *Fuji Lake Hotel*, *Wakakusa no Yado Maruei*, *Ooike Hotel*, *Hotel Koryu*).
     * **Urban Day-Use *Kashikiri* (No Overnight Stay Needed):** *Maenohara Onsen Saya-no-Yudokoro* (Tokyo Itabashi, ¥3,300/60m stone garden bath) and *Toyosu Senkyaku Banrai Manyo Club* (Tokyo Bay, hourly private baths + free rooftop footbath garden).

### Matrix C: Season, Weather & Holiday Bottleneck Rules

Check `arrival_date` and `departure_date` against Japan's seasonal calendar:

1. **Domestic Holiday & Saturday Bottleneck Detection:**
   * **Major Blackout Windows:** **Golden Week** (Apr 29 – May 5), **Obon** (mid-August), **New Year's (*Shogatsu*, Dec 29 – Jan 4)**, and **3-Day National Holiday Weekends** (e.g., *Culture Day* around Nov 3; *Labor Thanksgiving* around Nov 21–23; *Sports Day* in Oct; *Coming of Age Day* in Jan).
   * **Automated Bottleneck Fix:** If a multi-night hotel search in Kyoto, Tokyo, or an onsen town returns `<20%` availability or 2×–3× price surges, **run a day-by-day `sweepDays` price check** to isolate the single sold-out Saturday or holiday weekend. Offer the user a **reordered route** (e.g., visiting Kyoto on weekdays *before* the holiday weekend and placing Tokyo—which has 10× more business hotel inventory—over the holiday weekend).
2. **Seasonal Routing Adjustments:**
   * **Spring (*Sakura*, Late Mar – Mid Apr):** Book hotels and Shinkansen seats early; if arriving after peak bloom in Tokyo/Kyoto, route to higher elevations (**Mt. Yoshino**, **Fujikawaguchiko**, **Takayama**, **Kanazawa**, or **Tohoku**).
   * **Summer (Jun – Aug):** High heat/humidity + June rainy season (*tsuyu*). Route outdoor walks before 10:00 AM and after 5:00 PM; schedule museums, department stores (*depachika*), and covered arcades (*shotengai*) from 12:00–4:00 PM; highlight summer fireworks (*hanabi*) and festivals (*Gion Matsuri* in July, *Awa Odori* / *Nebuta* in Aug); suggest highland escapes (**Hokkaido**, **Kamikochi**, **Karuizawa**, **Nikko**).
   * **Autumn (*Koyo*, Mid Oct – Early Dec):** Track the foliage front by elevation (Hokkaido/Nikko/Alps in Oct → Kawaguchiko & Northern Kyoto [*Kifune/Kurama/Takao*] in mid-Nov → Central Kyoto [*Eikan-do/Kodai-ji/To-ji*] in late Nov → Tokyo [*Rikugien/Shinjuku Gyoen/Meiji Jingu Gaien*] in late Nov to early Dec). Always include **evening temple/garden illuminations**.
   * **Winter (Dec – Feb):** Crisp, clear skies (**highest Mt. Fuji visibility of the year!**). Route ski/snow travelers to **Niseko/Furano (Hokkaido)**, **Hakuba/Nozawa Onsen (Nagano)**, or **Gala Yuzawa** (75 mins by Shinkansen from Tokyo). If traveling over **Dec 29 – Jan 4 (*Shogatsu*)**, warn that independent restaurants and museums close for 4–5 days—book hotel/ryokan dinners and department store restaurants in advance.

### Matrix E: Dietary & Allergy Rules + Dynamic Chef Card Generator

Map `dietary_restrictions` to safe/unsafe Japanese dishes and generate a customized **Japanese Chef Card (`【食事制限のお願い】`)** in the final itinerary:

1. **Pescatarian (Fish/Seafood OK, No Land Meat):**
   * *Hidden Traps:* Pork bone (*tonkotsu*) and chicken (*tori paitan* / *chintan*) broths in ramen/udon; pork lard (*raado*) in gyoza and some tonkatsu/tempura shops; beef/pork tallow in Japanese curry blocks; *kamo* (duck) or *kashiwa* (chicken) in soba; bacon/ham in potato salad.
   * *Safe Bets:* Edomae sushi, sashimi, sesame-oil tempura, *unagi* (freshwater eel), *shojin ryori* (Buddhist temple cuisine), *yudofu* (tofu in kelp dashi), traditional *kombu + katsuobushi* soba, pure fish/clam-broth ramen (*Ramen Takahashi*, *Konjiki Hototogisu*), and vegan ramen (*T's Tantan*).
2. **Vegetarian & Vegan (No Meat, No Fish Dashi):**
   * *Hidden Traps:* **Bonito fish flakes (*katsuobushi*) and dried sardines (*niboshi*) are in 95% of Japanese soups, sauces, miso soup, and tamagoyaki.**
   * *Safe Bets:* **Zen Buddhist *Shojin Ryori*** (*Shigetsu* at Tenryu-ji, *Izusen* at Daitoku-ji), dedicated vegan/vegetarian restaurants via **HappyCow** (*T's Tantan* at Tokyo/Ueno/Ikebukuro stations, *Ain Soph*, *Vegan Ramen UZU Kyoto*, *Kyoto Engine Ramen*), *Inari-zushi* & vegetable rolls (*kappa-maki*, *kanpyo-maki*, *ume-shiso*), and custom *okonomiyaki* with kelp (*kombu*) stock.
3. **Halal / No Pork & No Alcohol:**
   * *Hidden Traps:* *Mirin* (sweet cooking wine) and *sake* in teriyaki/sushi rice/sauces; pork broth and lard in ramen; gelatin in desserts.
   * *Safe Bets:* Use **Halal Gourmet Japan (`halalgourmet.jp`)**; certified Halal Wagyu yakiniku/shabu-shabu, Halal ramen shops (*Ayam-Ya* Kyoto/Tokyo, *Honolu Ramen*, *Mazilu Lanzhou Beef Noodles*), Edomae sushi (request no mirin in *nikiri* brush sauce), and grilled fish (*yakizakana*).
4. **Gluten-Free / Celiac:**
   * *Hidden Traps:* Regular Japanese soy sauce (*shoyu*) contains wheat; *unagi* tare sauce, teriyaki, barley miso (*mugi-miso*), tempura batter, and standard *ni-hachi soba* (80% buckwheat / 20% wheat).
   * *Safe Bets:* **100% pure buckwheat soba (`十割そば` / *Juwari Soba*)** ordered with salt instead of dipping sauce, sashimi/nigiri sushi (bring a travel bottle of **Gluten-Free Tamari Soy Sauce**!), *yakitori* ordered **`shio` (salt-grilled)** instead of `tare` (soy glaze), and *shabu-shabu* with plain kombu broth.
5. **No Raw Fish / Seafood Allergy:**
   * *Safe Bets:* *Yakitori* (charcoal-grilled chicken skewers), *Tonkatsu* (breaded pork cutlets — *Maisen*, *Tonki*, *Katsukura*), *Wagyu Yakiniku / Sukiyaki / Teppanyaki*, *Gyukatsu*, *Ramen* (pork/chicken broth), *Okonomiyaki*, and *Kushikatsu*.

## 5. Interest-Tag Module Loader & Master Catalog of 300 Curated Experiences (`#JP-001` – `#JP-300`)

### A. How the Agent Selects Experiences from a User's `TripBrief`
When generating an itinerary, filter the **300 Curated Experiences** below using this 4-step scoring pipeline:
1. **Interest Match (`category_slug` + `interest_tags`):** Pull candidate experiences from the primary categories matching `trip_brief.interests`, plus any experience in other categories carrying a matching secondary tag (e.g., `pescatarian_friendly`, `vegan_vegetarian`, `hands_on_workshop`, `anti_crowd_dupe`, `off_the_beaten_path`, `family_friendly`, `indoor_rainy_day`, `tattoo_friendly`, `free_experience`).
2. **Geographic & Route Filter (`location`):** Keep experiences located in `trip_brief.must_visit_cities`, along the intercity Shinkansen/flight corridor between them, or within a 30–90 minute day-trip radius (`optional_day_trips`).
3. **Physical Pacing Filter (`step_intensity`):**
   * If `pacing_style == "relaxed"` (or `max_daily_steps <= 12000`): Schedule at most **1 `Moderate` experience** and **1–2 `Low` intensity experiences** per day (never schedule `High` intensity hikes/theme parks unless explicitly requested).
   * If `pacing_style == "balanced"`: Schedule up to **2 `Moderate`** + **1–2 `Low`** experiences per day.
   * If `pacing_style == "fast_paced"`: Schedule **`High` or `Moderate`** anchors starting at 07:00 AM.
4. **Season, Day-of-Week & Booking-Window Enforcement (`best_timing`):**
   * Verify that seasonal spectacles (`#JP-161`–`#JP-180`) match the user's travel month.
   * Respect closed days (e.g., Mondays at most museums and *Kakimori*; Tuesdays at *NACT* and *Teshima*; Wednesdays at *Weekenders Coffee* and *Kyoto Manga Museum*) and opening hours (never schedule *Nakano Broadway* watch/cel shops `#JP-206` / `#JP-241` before 11:30 AM).
   * Flag 30-to-60-day advance booking windows (*Ghibli Museum/Park*, *OMAKASE/TableCheck* fine dining, *USJ Express Passes*, *Enoura Observatory*, *Mazda Museum*, *Katsura Imperial Villa*).

## 6. Airport, Rail & Luggage Logistics Engine

### A. Airport-to-Hotel Alignment Rules
1. **Tokyo Haneda (`HND`):** Only 15–22 km from central Tokyo.
   * Best paired with **West Tokyo (Shinjuku / Shibuya / Shinagawa / Akasaka)** or **Ginza/Nihonbashi** (20–30 mins by taxi [~$45–$55], 30 mins by Keikyu/Monorail, or direct Limousine Bus).
2. **Tokyo Narita Terminals 1 & 2 (`NRT_T1_T2`):** 70 km east of Tokyo.
   * **Keisei Skyliner** (41 mins non-stop) → **Ueno / Nippori**.
   * **Narita Express (`N'EX`)** (55–75 mins) → **Tokyo Station, Shinagawa, Shibuya, Shinjuku**.
3. **Tokyo Narita Terminal 3 (`NRT_T3` — Low-Cost Carrier Terminal):**
   * **CRITICAL RULE:** Trains do **not** stop at Terminal 3 (passengers must walk 600m or take a shuttle from Terminal 2 with bags).
   * Always route `NRT_T3` departures from **East/Central Tokyo (`Nihonbashi / Hamacho / Ginza / Tokyo Station`)** via the **direct Airport Bus from `T-CAT` (Suitengumae, 55 mins)** or **Tokyo Station Yaesu South Exit (¥1,500, 65 mins)**, which drops passengers directly at the **curb of Terminal 3**.
4. **Kansai International (`KIX`) & Osaka Itami (`ITM`):**
   * `KIX` → **JR Haruka Limited Express** (75 mins direct to **Kyoto Station** or 35 mins to Osaka Tennoji/Shin-Osaka), or **Nankai Rapi:t** (38 mins to Osaka Namba).

### B. Intercity Rail & Luggage Forwarding (*Takkyubin*) Rules
1. **JR Pass Math Rule (Post-Price Hike):**
   * The national 7/14/21-day JR Pass **rarely pays off** for standard Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka round trips. Instruct travelers to buy individual Shinkansen tickets via **SmartEX (`smart-ex.jp`)** (and link them to their Apple Wallet Suica/Pasmo) or use **regional passes** (like the *JR Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass*) that still save money.
2. **Mt. Fuji Seat Side Rule on the Tokaido Shinkansen:**
   * Reserve **Seat E** (in standard 3+2 cars) or **Seat D** (in Green Car 2+2) for the Mt. Fuji window view (~40–45 mins after leaving Tokyo Station, or ~1h30m after leaving Kyoto Station).
3. **Shinkansen 160 cm Luggage Rule:**
   * Suitcases with `Length + Width + Height <= 160 cm` go on the overhead rack with no reservation. Suitcases between **161 cm and 250 cm** require selecting **"Seat with Oversized Baggage Area"** (free, books the back row of the car).
4. **Yamato Takkyubin (Luggage Forwarding) Trigger:**
   * Whenever an itinerary includes a 1-to-2-night intermediate stop in a mountain/onsen town (e.g., *Fujikawaguchiko*, *Hakone*, *Koyasan*, *Takayama*, *Kinosaki*, *Miyajima*), instruct the traveler to ship their main checked suitcases from **City A Hotel directly to City B Hotel** (~¥2,500 / $16 per bag via the hotel front desk) and travel to the intermediate stop with only a small overnight bag or carry-on.
